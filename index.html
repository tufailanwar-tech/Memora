Context is the architecture of recall.

Memory does not behave like a filing cabinet. It reconstructs meaning from cues, places, and relationships.

Remembering is not a simple replay of stored information. The mind rebuilds an experience, drawing on the details available in the present moment.

A familiar room, a phrase, or a question can all act as a cue. When those cues echo the conditions in which something was learned, the path back to it becomes easier to find.

How does context improve memory retrieval?

Context gives your brain overlapping cues to follow back to a memory. When the cues at recall match the learning environment, retrieval gets easier [1, 2].
